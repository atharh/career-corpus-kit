Inherits every dimension in `_manager-core.md`. What follows is the engineering overlay only —
the craft bar this function keeps at every level, director included.

## Scope by level

The core file's rows hold. What is specific here is what the objective is made of:

| | Made of |
|---|---|
| **Engineering manager** | a team's delivery against quarterly goals, its quality, and its on-call load |
| **Senior engineering manager** | several teams or workstreams, the operating model they ship on, and the architecture that spans them |
| **Engineering director** | a strategic objective with a technical shape — a platform, a migration, a capability the company did not have — plus its portfolio and budget |

## Dimensions — the engineering overlay

- **Craft, retained.** A design reviewed, a technical risk named early enough to matter, a
  system-health read, a competitive or vendor fact that changed a plan. Management ladders keep
  this bar at every level; an EM story with no technical substance reads as someone who stopped
  being an engineer, which no framework asks for.
- **A technical decision they owned rather than delegated**, with what made it theirs — a
  tradeoff between cost, quality and time-to-market that only the person holding the budget
  could resolve.
- **Delivery health as a measured thing.** Deployment frequency, lead time, change failure
  rate, review latency, incident load — the numbers that say whether the team could ship, as
  distinct from whether it shipped.
- **The reliability trade.** What was accepted as risk, what was paid down, and who was told.
  Including a time the trade was wrong.
- **Technical debt argued in business terms**, and whether it got funded. The clearest
  senior-manager tell in this craft: representing an engineering concern in the language of the
  people who allocate.
- **Platform or capability investment justified against a business objective**, with the
  counterfactual — what the org would have done instead, and what that would have cost.

## Named artifacts

Everything in the core file, plus: architecture review and its decisions, technical strategy
memo, migration or platform plan, incident review and its systemic actions, delivery metrics
dashboard, on-call health report, build-versus-buy analysis, vendor evaluation, the funding
memo that translated a technical need into a business case.

## How stories in this craft overclaim

Everything in the core file, plus: **the team's engineering rendered in the manager's voice.**
An EM describing the design their staff engineer produced as their own technical judgment is
the most common inflation in this role, and the most easily caught — an interviewer asks one
implementation question and the seam opens. The defensible version names what the manager
actually contributed: the constraint, the decision rights, the resourcing, the veto.

## Pillar blind spots

Everything in the core file. The overlay above exists to close the **craft** gap; the
**results** gap stays open unless the story carries the business number, not just the delivery
number.
