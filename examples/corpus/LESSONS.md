# LESSONS — personal calibrations for the career-corpus skills

The skills read this at session start and treat each entry as a rule for working with **this**
corpus, on equal footing with their built-in rules. Appended when a correction generalises.
Newest at the bottom. One lesson each.

A lesson leaves two ways, each on your explicit say-so, entry by entry. One that proves wrong is
struck in place, never rewritten — `~~<the entry>~~ retired YYYY-MM-DD: <one clause of why>` —
and is no longer applied. One the kit's method has since fully absorbed is deleted outright, once
nothing local remains in it (no calibration about you, no number you set, no recorded
divergence; trim the entry to that residue if any does) and no session still loads a kit version
without the rule.

**This file is the whole personalisation mechanism.** It ships empty. It fills up with the
things you had to tell Claude twice.

---

- 2026-02-14 — **Ask what a claim did, not what it was called.** Sam described the Bellhaven
  work as "a data platform", then "basically a data mesh", and both dissolved the moment they
  were asked what it actually ran. It was a cron job and a second Postgres. The modern word
  arrives automatically and honestly — it isn't puffery, it's just how people describe old work
  now — so the fix is a mechanical question, not suspicion. See the `anachronisms_corrected`
  block in `bellhaven/reporting-migration.md`.

- 2026-02-14 — **"Led the team" is never right for Sam. "Led the work" usually is.** They have
  never had reports and they will correct any draft that blurs it, quickly and slightly testily.
  This is a ceiling, not modesty — an EM interviewer establishes it in one question.

- 2026-03-02 — **Sam's own number is lower than their résumé's number, every time so far.**
  "80%" became "about half"; "the org adopted it" became "two teams". Both times the correction
  came unprompted and against their own interest. **Prefer their spoken figure over the written
  one**, and when they shrink a claim, write the smaller version in as a ceiling immediately so
  a later session can't quietly restore the bigger one.

- 2026-03-02 — **Don't dress up why they fixed something.** Claude framed the batch work as
  spotting an architectural blind spot; Sam's actual reason was that the pager kept waking them
  up. They rejected the flattering version flatly. **The plain motive is more credible and they
  will always pick it** — render the honest one and let the result do the work.
